# Estrangement — The Taxonomy of Moves

*Estrangement (defamiliarization) is making the familiar strange enough to be seen again. The trick is to do it with a rule, not a whim. One rule changed, held without flinching, is what separates the uncanny from the random.*

## The discipline before the moves

Before you estrange anything, **name the real subject in one plain sentence.** "This is a waitlist teaser for a calm-tech sleep device." "This is a deck-restoration before/after." You cannot return *one true thing* about a subject you never named, and you cannot tell whether a rule-change *illuminates* the subject or just decorates it.

Then pick **one** move below. The amateur instinct is to stack five weirdnesses; the result reads as noise. One altered law, fully committed, beats five half-committed ones every time.

---

## The five core moves

### 1. Rule-swap — change one law of how the world works
Take a single rule of reality and invert or replace it. *Dreams are currency. Silence is taxed. Memory is a physical material you can run out of. Light is something you owe.* The subject then lives in a world organized around that swapped law, and its meaning re-renders.

- **Best for:** launch teasers, manifestos, anything where you want a whole worldview implied in one line.
- **Illuminates when:** the swapped rule is secretly *true* of the subject's category (a sleep app in a world where "rest is the only currency that doesn't inflate").
- **Fails as:** a rule nobody can feel the consequences of. If you can't immediately derive three downstream facts from the swap, it's decoration.

### 2. Scale-shift — change the size or zoom of attention
Treat the tiny as monumental or the vast as intimate. *The dust on a screen as a continent. A single notification as a weather system. A user's hesitation rendered at geological scale.* Micro-to-macro and macro-to-micro both defamiliarize by breaking the reader's default zoom.

- **Best for:** carousels and sequences (you can travel the scale panel by panel), product detail that's normally ignored.
- **Illuminates when:** the shift reveals stakes the normal scale hides (the "small" decision that's actually enormous).
- **Fails as:** arbitrary bigness. Scale for awe alone, with no insight at the new zoom, is empty spectacle.

### 3. Animacy-shift — change what is alive, aware, or willing
Grant interiority to the inanimate, or strip it from the animate. *The device is alive and slightly bored. The spreadsheet has opinions. The old deck remembers every party it held. The user is treated as weather, not a person.* The object gets a point of view; the world tilts.

- **Best for:** product-as-character, brand mascotry that isn't cute, animist/dream pillars.
- **Illuminates when:** the object's "feelings" are really the *user's* projected feelings handed back to them (the device is bored *because the user is*).
- **Fails as:** Pixar-lamp cuteness — anthropomorphism with no edge. Give it a strange want, not a friendly face.

### 4. Time-shift — change when, or how, time runs
Report from a future that already happened, a past that hasn't, or a present where time behaves wrong. *Artifacts from the coming collapse. A product reviewed by archaeologists. Graffiti from a timeline that diverged. The launch remembered as ancient history.* Future-artifact and forgotten-relic are the two workhorses here.

- **Best for:** "relic from the future" aesthetics, teasers, anything wanting inevitability ("this already won, you're just catching up").
- **Illuminates when:** the time-frame reveals what *endures* about the subject (what survives to be excavated is what mattered).
- **Fails as:** sci-fi set dressing. "In the year 2099…" with no consequence is a costume, not a move.

### 5. Perspective-from-the-object — report from a non-human vantage
Don't describe the object; *become* it, or stand where it stands. *The dispatch is written by the product, or by the room, or by the thing being measured.* This is the purest form of "report from inside" — the vantage itself is the estrangement.

- **Best for:** voice-forward weird, first-person mystery, copy that should feel overheard rather than addressed.
- **Illuminates when:** the non-human vantage notices what humans are too close to see.
- **Fails as:** a gimmick narrator with nothing to observe. The vantage must *see differently*, not just *speak oddly*.

## Choosing the move that illuminates

Run two quick tests on a candidate move:

1. **The downstream test.** From the rule-change, can you derive at least three consistent facts about the world *for free*? If the move is generative, the world unfolds itself. If you have to force every detail, the move is shallow — switch axes.
2. **The lens test.** Finish this sentence: "Seen through this world, the real subject is revealed to be ______." If you can't fill the blank with something *true and slightly uncomfortable*, you have spectacle, not estrangement. Go back.

**Default move when the brief gives no direction:** invert the category's single most boring assumption. Every category has a tired given everyone repeats; rule-swap *that*, and the work stops the scroll because it argues with the thing the audience stopped noticing.

## Combining moves (carefully)

You may run a **primary + secondary** move — but the secondary must obey the primary's rule, not introduce a competing one. Example: *time-shift* primary (a future archaeologist's field notes) + *animacy-shift* secondary (the excavated device still faintly wants something). That holds, because the animacy lives *inside* the time-frame. What breaks: two rule-swaps at once — now there are two physics and the reader can't find footing. **One world, one law. A second move is allowed only as a citizen of the first world, never as a rival to it.**

## Output discipline

When generating N candidates, vary the *move* across them — don't write five rule-swaps. A good candidate set is: one rule-swap, one animacy-shift, one time-shift (say), each voiced in a fitting dialect, so the approval step can learn *which axis of strangeness this loop actually rewards*. That variance is the training signal; sameness across candidates wastes the generation.
