# Worldbuilding — Reality Design

*Worldbuilding isn't narrative. It's reality design. Brands that worldbuild don't just tell stories — they shift the coordinates of culture. This file is for when the estrangement rule is chosen and the world needs enough depth that you can hold it consistently across one piece — and across many.*

## From storytelling to worldbuilding

Storytelling is about *what* a brand says. Worldbuilding is about *where* the story takes place, and *who gets to live there*. The shift in every dimension:

| Storytelling | Worldbuilding |
|---|---|
| Speaks to a **customer** | Speaks to an **inhabitant** |
| Clarity, empathy, persuasion | Immersion, curiosity, enchantment |
| Narrative, identity, message | Lore, ritual, mythology, symbolic artifacts |
| Structured for understanding | Designed for exploration |
| Answers "What do we say?" | Answers "What world are we inviting them into?" |

When you report from inside an alien world (core method step 3), you are speaking to an *inhabitant* of that world — someone who already accepts its rules. That posture is what makes the dispatch feel overheard and real rather than pitched.

## Why worldbuilding creates pull

A built world behaves like a **gravity field** — a cultural black hole. Dense. Warped. Inescapably attractive. People don't just buy in; they move in, dress the part, speak the language, and spread the lore. This is the gravity that complements Alien's interference: the strangeness stops the scroll (interference), the world keeps them there (gravity).

## The building blocks of a world

Once the estrangement rule sets the world's single altered law, give the world enough texture to be *held* — but only the texture a given piece needs. The materials:

- **Lore** — the world's history and givens. What everyone in this world already knows and never explains. (You imply lore; you never info-dump it.)
- **Ritual** — repeated actions the inhabitants perform. Rituals make a world feel *lived in* rather than described.
- **Mythology** — the world's sacred logic; what it reveres, fears, owes.
- **Symbolic artifacts** — objects that carry the world's meaning. The product, reframed as an artifact of this world, is the most important one.
- **Inhabitants** — who lives here, and how they speak (this is where `dialects.md` plugs in).

You do not need all five in any single post. A scroll-stopper might be pure ritual glimpsed sideways; a teaser might be a single artifact with implied lore. **Imply the iceberg, show the tip.** The reader feeling there's *more world than they were shown* is the pull.

## Holding consistency across many posts (the world as compounding asset)

A world is not a one-off costume — it's an asset that compounds, exactly like the loop's substrate. Each post should obey the *same* altered law and add to the *same* lore, so that over weeks the audience learns the world's physics and the world gains the density that creates gravity. Practical discipline:

- **One altered law per world**, held across every post in that world. (Different campaigns can run different worlds; within a world, the law is fixed.)
- **Accrete, don't reset.** A new post can reveal new lore, but never contradict established lore. Contradiction is the same failure as breaking the rule mid-post — it tells the audience the world is fake.
- **Recurring artifacts and rituals** are how a world becomes recognizable. Reuse them; let them gain meaning through repetition (this is alternating repetition doing brand work).
- **The product is the central artifact.** Every world this engine builds exists to make the real subject *legible and desirable* from a strange angle. If the world drifts away from the subject, it stops being a lens and becomes a distraction — pull it back.

## Worldbuilding in service of the one true thing

Reality design is seductive; it's easy to fall in love with the world and forget the subject. The world is always a **lens on the real one** (core method step 4). Before a world ships, confirm: *inhabiting this world makes the audience understand or feel something true about the actual product.* If the world is gorgeous but says nothing true about the subject, you've built a beautiful empty set. The gravity field has to pull people toward the real thing.
