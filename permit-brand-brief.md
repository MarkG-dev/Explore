# Permit → Brand Strategy Brief
### Contractor lead intelligence, built on permit data

_Product = monitors public building-permit filings and turns them into exclusive, first-mover leads
for contractors. Permit pulled → money committed → job → the contractor who knows first wins. The
brand's job: sell that head start in language a contractor respects, and escape the "shared-lead
scam" stigma that poisons the whole category._

---

## 1. The big decision: keep "Permit" or rename?

This is a genuine keep-or-kill call, not a slam dunk either way. Here's the honest balance for a
**lead-intelligence** product specifically (the verdict would be different for a payments tool).

**Arguments FOR keeping "Permit":**
- **It owns the trigger event.** Permit filing is, in the category's own words, *"the clearest trigger
  event in construction"* and *"the first signal of construction"* (Shovels). Naming yourself after the
  signal every competitor is chasing is a first-mover flag-plant.
- **The red-tape hate doesn't stick to you.** Contractors resent the permit *process* — the inspector,
  the fee, the wait. You're not making them do any of that; you hand them the *output*. The baggage sits
  on City Hall, not on a data brand.
- **It's insider language + a legitimacy badge.** "Pull a permit" is real trade vocabulary outsiders
  don't use, and "good contractors pull permits" (A-list vs. C-list) gives the word quiet respectability.

**Arguments AGAINST:**
- **Category ambiguity is the real risk** — not bureaucracy vibes. Cold, "Permit" reads as a permit-
  *expediting/compliance* tool (Permits.com = "Online Permit Expediter"; PermitFlow). You'd fight to not
  be mistaken for the opposite of what you are.
- **The namespace is a pile-up:** PermitGrab, PermitLedger, PermitMap, PermitVector, PermitStack,
  Permits.com — weak trademark distinctiveness and brutal SEO.
- **It names the source, not the value.** The contractor wants *the job*, told *first*, *just to them*.

**Recommendation:** Keep "Permit" **only if** you (a) lock a lead-intelligence descriptor permanently
beside it so it never reads as a compliance tool — e.g. *"Permit — know before your competition"* — and
(b) can clear a distinctive mark despite the crowd. Otherwise, move to a name that says the *value* (§6).
Either way, **own the verb, not the noun**: *you're cleared, you're first* — never paperwork-and-delay.

## 2. Competitive map (your real category)

| Player | What it is | Tagline (as indexed) | Price feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shovels.ai** | API / dev-first permit data | *"The intelligence layer for the built world"* | Free tier; **$599/mo** |
| **Construction Monitor** | Weekly permit lists since '92 | *"Do you know who is building in your area? We do…"* | Territory subs (e.g. LA $96/mo–$960/yr) |
| **BuildZoom** | Marketplace + permit data | *"A Better Way to Build"* | Free + 2.5% success fee; data $500–2,000/mo |
| **Building Radar** | Enterprise sales intel (EU) | *"Revenue Engineering… first-mover advantage"* | Custom enterprise |
| **Dodge / ConstructConnect / BidClerk** | Legacy commercial bid leads | *"Intelligence that plugs in, not piles on"* / *"Construction Starts Here"* | $1k–$3k+/yr, annual lock-in |
| **PermitMap** (FL) | Weekly permit leads, local | *"No shared leads, no bidding wars, no middleman"* | Cheap local SaaS |
| **Permit Ledger** | Renovation permit leads | Renovation-only dashboard | **$39/mo, no contract** |
| **Angi / HomeAdvisor / Thumbtack** | Shared lead marketplace | — | $25–$100+/lead, **the villain** |

**The market splits two ways:** enterprise permit data ($300–$2,000+/mo, annual, national, developer/
supplier-oriented) vs. cheap local permit SaaS ($39/mo, renovation-filtered). **Shared vulnerabilities:**
leads overlap with free public records, rigid non-refundable annual contracts, and — for Angi et al. —
total loss of trust. The **trusted, contractor-native, exclusive-and-local** position is largely unowned.

## 3. Positioning — build it against shared leads

The category's emotional center of gravity is **betrayal by lead-gen.** Own the opposite.

> **The unfair head start. A permit means the money's already committed — we hand you the job before
> your competition knows it exists. One contractor per lead. Never resold. Never a bidding war.**

Three pillars, each a direct answer to a documented grievance:
1. **First** — *"78% go with the first contractor to respond."* You know before the homeowner even shops.
2. **Yours** — the #1 rage point is *"the same lead sold to 4 other guys."* Yours alone, or you don't pay.
3. **Real** — a pulled permit = committed money, not a *"they were just shopping"* tire-kicker.

## 4. Voice-of-customer ammunition (verbatim, for copy & sales)

The anti-Angi rage — this is the enemy you position against:
- *"I would never pay somebody for a lead when they are openly telling me they are going to give that
  same lead to 2–4 other contractors… and we have to fight over it."* (Electrician Talk)
- *"Legal scam."* / *"Angie's List and Home Advisor practice should be illegal."* (ContractorTalk)
- *"Bogus leads… bad numbers, never respond… some ask for services you don't even offer."*
- *"Most contractors burn through $5–10K learning what doesn't work first."*
- The unmet demand, stated plainly: *"Is there ANY lead company that isn't a scam?"* (Quora)

Speed-to-lead is already how they think (gold for the name):
- *"Storm chasers,"* *"first to the door,"* *"speed to lead,"* *"dumpsters in driveways,"*
  *"shingle-heavy neighborhoods,"* *"the first contractor to respond wins."*

General money/ethos register (ContractorTalk / HVAC-Talk): *"Be ruthless, they are stealing from you,"*
*"Hell hath no fury like a contractor stiffed,"* *"just want simple,"* *"$100 a month for what?"*

**Write like this.** Blunt, plain, money-first. Never a SaaS landing page.

## 5. The blue-collar brand-voice playbook (steal these patterns)

From the brands contractors actually respect:
- **Short, all-caps, unqualified promise on ONE virtue.** Milwaukee *"Nothing but HEAVY DUTY."* DeWalt
  *"GUARANTEED TOUGH."* Your virtue: *first / yours / real.*
- **Insider flattery — they're the pro.** Ridgid *"Built For Those Who Know."* A permit-intel tool
  literally makes them *the one who knows first.* Lean in.
- **Frame the cost of failure in their economics.** Klein: failure *"costs time, money and reputation."*
  Yours: *the job you never heard about went to the other guy.*
- **Say "money," kill euphemism.** Cash App *"cash in."* Ramp *"Time is money."* Gusto *"No math. No stress."*
- **Imperative verbs, like a coworker.** Venmo *"Get paid. Get growing. Get ahead."* Yours: *Get there first.*
- **Anti-corporate by restraint** (Carhartt, Square). Plainness = honesty — vital when the category is
  seen as slick and extractive.

**Naming conventions to avoid:** the saturated morphemes `Job-` (Jobber/JobNimbus/JobTread/Joist),
`Build-`, `Field-`/`Service-`, worn-out `Pro` suffix, and dropped-vowel `-ify` SaaS names (Roofr, Workiz)
— the exact "Silicon Valley" cliché a trades buyer distrusts. Open lane: short, real, concrete words.

## 6. Names — three tiers (if you move off "Permit")

### Tier 1 — Investable but blunt
- **Bird Dog** ⭐ — "bird-dogging" is the insider term for sniffing out deals and pointing you to them;
  bird-dog *fees* = money-for-leads. Ethos + function + money in one word.
  → *"Your permit bird dog. It points, you close."*
- **Backlog** — a full backlog is money in the bank; pure trade vocabulary. → *"Keep the backlog full."*
- **Frontrunner** — ahead of the pack, faint finance edge. → *"Get there first. Close it first."*

### Tier 2 — Contractor-ethos, plain (validated by "first-to-respond wins")
- **First Knock** ⭐ — first contractor to knock gets the job; the entire value in two words, and the
  data says speed-to-lead literally wins. → *"First knock wins."*
- **Dibs** — call dibs before anyone knows it's up; ownable, a little cocky. → *"Call dibs on the job."*
- **Heads Up / The Jump** — trade-native. → *"Heads up on every job in your zip."*

### Tier 3 — Spicy, money-forward
- **Payday** — permit hits → payday. Boldest anti-Angi swing that still reads as a real brand.
  → *"Every permit is somebody's payday. Make it yours."*
- **Bloodhound** — sniffs jobs out of public data; tough. → *"Sniffs out the money before anyone else."*
- **Grubstake** — old builder/prospector word for the stake that gets you working; rugged, insider.

---

## 7. Recommendation

Two clean paths:

- **Keep "Permit"** — defensible *if* you nail a permanent lead-intel descriptor and clear a distinctive
  mark. Lead line: **"Permit — know before your competition. One job. One contractor. Never resold."**
- **Rename for the value** — lead with **Bird Dog** or **First Knock** (both on-ethos, self-explaining,
  money-blunt, and free of the crowded "Permit-" pile). Boldest swing: **Payday.**

Whichever wordmark wins, the tagline should hit the DeWalt-flat cadence and name the Angi villain:
> **Real jobs. One contractor. No recycled leads.**

## 8. Next steps
- Trademark + .com check on the shortlist (Bird Dog, First Knock, Payday) and on "Permit" as a distinct mark.
- 5-contractor gut-check: 3 names + taglines → *"which sounds like it was built by someone who's held a
  hammer?"* and *"does this sound like a scam lead company?"* (trust is the whole battleground).
- Lock the positioning line before the name — the name serves "the unfair head start."
