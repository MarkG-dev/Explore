# Acquisition Memo — Brand-Transformation Targets

**Prepared:** June 10, 2026 · **For:** solo SBA 7(a) buyer (~$250K equity, NYC/Northeast)
**Buy-box:** $1.0M–$2.5M EV · $350K–$700K SDE · 10–20+ yr history
**Companion file:** `Brand-Transformation_Acquisition_Pipeline.xlsx` (Tab 1 listings · Tab 2 outreach universe · Tab 3 dormant IP · Tab 4 precedents/comps)

> **Two ground-truth caveats up front.** (1) Every business-for-sale marketplace and broker site (BizBuySell, BizQuest, LoopNet, DealStream, Synergy, BusinessesForSale) blocks automated access — so **no live-listing financial is verified**; Tab 1 numbers are snippet-level and require a broker NDA to confirm. (2) The desk research surfaced a hard structural fact that should reframe expectations: **at the $350–700K SDE floor, the on-market pipeline in all three verticals is thin-to-empty.** The median awards shop, heritage maker, and hobby publisher throws off **$100–250K SDE**, below your floor. This is a **proprietary-outreach thesis, not a browse-the-listings thesis.** That is a finding, not a failure — it tells you where to spend the next 90 days.

## 1. The financing reality (sets the whole strategy)

With ~$250K cash under the **June 2025 SOP 50 10 8** rules: minimum equity injection is **10% of total project cost**, and a seller note only counts toward it if on **full 10-year standby** (covering at most half the injection).

| Scenario | Clean with $250K cash? |
|---|---|
| **$1.5M EV** (~$195–225K all-in) | ✅ Comfortable, with buffer |
| **$2.0M EV** (~$260–300K all-in) | ⚠️ Realistic ceiling on an all-cash injection |
| **$2.5M EV** | Only with a cooperative 10-yr-standby seller note |

Multiples are internally consistent: small manufacturers and these niches trade at **~3x SDE**, so **$350–700K SDE → ~$1.05M–$2.1M EV** — squarely in-box. **Underwrite the $1.5–2.0M EV zone as the base case.** The top of the range requires seller cooperation you cannot assume.

## 2. Top 5 overall targets

These are the highest-conviction names across both the for-sale and outreach pools, ranked by screen score and edge-fit.

**1. Guillow's (Paul K. Guillow, Inc.) — Vertical C — Wakefield, MA — score 12/12.**
Century-old (1926) family balsa-flying-model maker, hitting its **100-year milestone in 2026** as a self-described "mom-and-pop." Frozen brand (staleness 5), in-house production, **consumable repeat demand** (kits are consumed and rebought), mass + hobby + wholesale distribution, and a textbook succession profile. The buyer's edge maps directly: a heritage-kit reposition from a $15–25 commodity kit to a $40–120 design object (the Liberty/Areaware multiple). *Risk: a beloved, possibly emotionally-held family asset — seller-readiness is the open question; price/SDE entirely unverified.*

**2. Widdy Dart Board Mfg. — Vertical B — Philadelphia, PA — score 12/12.**
1910 family maker of iconic American-style dartboards and darts. Niche-dominant, **consumable demand** (boards wear out, darts rebought, league channel), zero modern brand work (staleness 5), Northeast. This is the cleanest "solved making + dormant brand + recurring demand + succession" stack found, with a credible bar/game-room DTC + premium-gifting second act. *Risk: small absolute size (likely sub-$3M revenue, possibly sub-floor SDE) and single-product concentration; verify owner age and that production isn't dependent on one aging craftsman.*

**3. Pen & Trophy Center — Vertical A — East Syracuse, NY — score 11/12.**
Owned by the Monti family **since 1948**; founder-era owner with a single named successor, a **75-year corporate B2B book**, in-house engraving, and a stale web presence. The deepest recurring-corporate relationship base in the awards set, in-region. *Risk: revenue likely $0.6–1.2M (below SDE floor) → this is an anchor-or-bolt-on, not a standalone $2M deal; corporate-vs-walk-in mix needs confirming.*

**4. Channel Craft — Vertical C — Charleroi, PA — score 11/12.**
1981 "Crafted in USA" games/heritage-toy maker selling into **museums, specialty toy/gift, catalogs, and department stores** — the single best specialty-wholesale channel asset in the pipeline, and exactly the distribution that takes years to build. Founder-era ownership. *Risk: ~70 employees is real operating weight and runs against your stated weakness (heavy-ops management); confirm SDE and labor dependence before this clears.*

**5. S & S Trophy Inc. — Vertical A — Pittsburgh, PA — score 11/12.**
~58-year-old shop with in-house engraving + embroidery/silkscreen and **government contracts** (recurring, sticky), ownership not publicly disclosed (succession-opaque = often the most motivated). In-region. *Risk: government concentration could trip the >50%-one-customer exclusion — confirm the contract book is diversified; financials unverified.*

*Honorable mentions:* **Bristol Bronze (RI) + J.M. Reineck (MA)** as a two-shop **marine-hardware roll-up** (both staleness 5, both succession-acute); **Model Expo/Model Shipways (FL)** as the niche-dominant model-kit maker if you'll go out-of-region; **Reaper Miniatures (TX)** to ride the miniature-painting boom; the **DealStream "100+ yr, $1.2M SDE" listing (#jsza60)** as the one on-market item whose disclosed economics actually approach the band (though SDE sits above it).

## 3. Per-vertical buyability verdict (at this check size — stated plainly)

**Vertical A — Corporate Awards & Recognition: BUYABLE via OUTREACH, NOT via listings.**
On-market: ~10–12 shops nationwide at any time, only **three in the Northeast**, **zero with confirmed in-box financials** (the one with disclosed numbers was $128K SDE — half your floor). But the proprietary universe is **real and buildable**: an estimated 150–400+ independent NE shops, ~40–80 matching all criteria, ~11 named here in a single research pass. The catch: the median shop is **$400K–$1.5M revenue / $100–250K SDE**, below your floor. **Verdict: yes — but as an anchor-plus-tuck-in roll-up** (buy one $1.5M+ corporate-heavy shop like Paul's or Schoppy's, tuck in retiring owners around it), not a single standalone hit. Best brokered channel: **Synergy Business Brokers** (NE-focused, $250K+ net-income filter).

**Vertical B — Heritage Maker: BUYABLE, PUREST EDGE-FIT, ENTIRELY OFF-MARKET.**
This is where the buyer's skills create the most value (Faribault and Stanley prove an 18–24-month narrative+channel revival), and where almost nothing is listed. The targets are **owner-operated, succession-aged, commodity/OEM-priced, staleness-4/5** makers reachable only through an MNI/Thomasnet/Made-in-state screen. **Verdict: yes, and highest-upside — but it is a build-the-list-and-knock-on-doors campaign**, 6–18 months. Watch the kill criteria hard here: **environmental remediation (metal finishing), a single irreplaceable 70+ craftsman, and capex walls** are the real risks. The dormant-trademark lane (Tab 3) is a genuine lower-cost variant, but verify USPTO ownership first — several "famous" marks are alive inside conglomerates.

**Vertical C — Sleepy Hobby/Toy/Game: BUYABLE via OUTREACH; succession is firing NOW.**
Only ~2,600 toy/game *manufacturers* exist nationally, so listings are a trickle — but the **archetype is abundant and aging out**: BlueJacket Shipcrafters sold Oct 2024 (owner mid-70s), Stave's founder sold at 81, Guillow's is a century-old mom-and-pop. Northeast density is good (MA, VT, ME, NY). The buyer's edge maps directly onto under-monetized frozen brands with 3–10x pricing headroom. **Verdict: yes — the best-fit-plus-tailwind vertical — but a 6–18-month proprietary sourcing effort.** The best-known names (Thames & Kosmos, Estes, Liberty) are already taken or too big; the deals are in the **lower-profile second tier**.

**One-line synthesis:** All three are buyable, none via casual listing-browsing. **Vertical B has the most edge-fit upside; Vertical C has the most active succession + tailwind; Vertical A has the most named, in-region, build-ready targets today.**

## 4. Recommended 90-day sourcing sequence

**Days 0–15 — Infrastructure & the one live thread.**
- Buy the cheap prospecting rails: an **APA (Awards & Personalization Association) retail membership** for its gated retailer directory, and an **MNI/IndustrySelect state register** (start with NY + the DealStream/Thomasnet screens). Together these convert the universe from "estimated" to "named."
- Call the broker on **BizBuySell #2443951** (21-yr board-game IP brand) and **DealStream #jsza60** (100+ yr, $1.2M SDE) — the only two on-market items worth an NDA.

**Days 15–45 — Build the named list (target: 80–120 rows).**
- **Vertical A:** mine the APA directory NY/NJ/CT/PA/MA + Google-Maps sweep + Insights "Retailer of the Year" archives → finish the 40–80 NE shop list.
- **Vertical B:** run the MNI screen (pre-1985, <50 employees, consumer SIC codes 3421/3423/3645/3991/3949/3821/2393) + Made-in-NYC/NY + state MEP directories + Thomasnet "Made in USA / Custom Manufacturer" by category → 50-row sheet.
- **Vertical C:** diff **GAMA Expo / Toy Fair (mapyourshow) / iHobby 2008–2014 exhibitor archives** against current rosters — vanished-but-still-resolving names = dormancy signals.

**Days 45–75 — Score, rank, and open outreach.**
- Apply the /12 screen; cut anything <8 and anything tripping a kill criterion.
- Launch a **direct-owner approach campaign** (warm letter → call) to the staleness-4/5 succession-acute names: Guillow's, Widdy, Pen & Trophy, S&S, Channel Craft, Maple Landmark, Bristol Bronze + J.M. Reineck (as a pair), Reaper, the Maine lantern makers.
- In parallel, pull **USPTO status + ownership** for the six Tab 3 dormant marks.

**Days 75–90 — Convert to LOIs / diligence.**
- Engage **Synergy Business Brokers** for proprietary NE awards/consumer-mfg flow.
- Target **2–4 NDA'd conversations** and **1 LOI** by day 90. Prioritize the anchor-roll-up candidate in A and the single best heritage maker in B/C.

## 5. The three diligence questions that matter most, per vertical

**Vertical A — Awards & Recognition**
1. **What % of revenue is recurring corporate/institutional program work vs. seasonal youth-sports walk-in?** (>60% sports = kill; this single number decides the deal.)
2. **Is production genuinely in-house, and how owner-dependent is it?** (No in-house = reseller = kill; one aging operator running the laser = key-person risk.)
3. **How concentrated and how old are the house accounts?** (Confirm no single account >50%, and that the 10-yr+ relationships are contractual/standing-PO, not personal to the seller.)

**Vertical B — Heritage Maker**
1. **What is the environmental and equipment liability?** (Metal-finishing/plating remediation and >$200K near-term capex are the deal-killers here — get a Phase I and a machine-age audit.)
2. **Is the "making" documented, or does it live in one 70+ craftsman's head?** (Undocumented tribal knowledge = the moat evaporates at close.)
3. **Is the brand/trademark clean and owned by the seller?** (Confirm USPTO ownership, no licensing encumbrances, and that provenance claims are defensible.)

**Vertical C — Hobby/Toy/Game**
1. **Where is the tooling, and do you control it contractually?** (Overseas molds with no contractual control = you don't own the product.)
2. **What share of asking price is inventory, and is the IP/license owned outright?** (Inventory >40% of price = you're buying a warehouse; licensed-IP dependence (Disney/anime) dies with the deal.)
3. **Are there CPSIA / children's-product compliance gaps?** (Flag and price the testing debt — don't necessarily kill, but quantify it.)
